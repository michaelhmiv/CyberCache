# Crafting

Crafting is not a menu you visit between fights. It is an
**occupation** — one of the [[Actions and Occupations|actions]] your
hunter can be doing — and the Workshop is where the work happens. A
dedicated crafter is a real playstyle: when the market is hungry for
crafted goods, the hunter at the bench out-earns the hunter in the
field.

## The one rule: jobs run only while you craft

Workshop jobs advance **only while crafting is your active action**.
Queue a job, switch to roaming or a boss, and the job sits **paused** —
it keeps its place and its escrowed inputs, but no progress is made
until you come back to the bench. There is **one bench**: one job
runs on it, with up to **5 more queued** behind it (a Fixer keystone
queues more). A full bench says so on the recipe card — and names
the way out: jobs finish while Crafting is your action, or cancel
one from the job list.

Crafting pays **XP at about 77.5% of roaming's rate** for the same time
(the exact rate is pegged to the best roaming area unlocked at your
level, and a rushed job pays proportionally less because it took less
time). That is deliberately below roaming — and deliberately close
enough that supplying the market beats roaming's raw output whenever
demand is real.

**Everyone can craft, guests included.** Crafting carries no
verified-account gate.

## Finding what you can make

The Workshop opens on the catalog, sorted by **readiness**:

- **Craftable now** — materials in hand, every gate met. The
  **Craftable now** chip at the top of the page carries the live
  count and filters to just these recipes; **All 88** shows the
  whole catalog, and search covers recipe **names, jobs, and
  materials**.
- **One gate away** — exactly one kind of blocker stands between you
  and the bench, and the card names it.
- **Locked** — two or more kinds of blockers; the card prints every
  one of them.

Above the list, two strips print the numbers the gates read: your
**standing** (Mastery per lane, and your Production Systems research
state) and **the Bench** card — the one bench, always ready, with
the craft relics listed as its permanent modifiers (see below).

A recipe card's face is lean: the item, its lane, its time, its
materials with have/need counts, the totals for the job you are
about to queue, and the queue controls. Anything short prints
**where it comes from** right on its line — the roaming area that
drops it, the recipe that refines it, the Vendor rotation, or the
[[The Market]] — each one a door. Non-material blockers (level,
mastery, research, standing) print as lock lines on the card;
a shortage is never repeated in a banner, and the queue button
simply reads **QUEUE JOB**, greyed when the lines above it say no.
The **info button** beside a recipe's name opens its detail sheet:
what the item is for, its quality range and what the percentage
means for that item, its effects, and the full gate and material
detail.

**Quick buy.** A material or essence line that is short carries one
small **+** button. Tapping it opens the quick-buy sheet: pick how many you need — it
opens at exactly your shortfall — and the sheet prices the fill on
two tabs, **Market** (the cheapest player listings first) and
**Vendor** (only when the vendor stocks that good today), each tab
showing its total. The price you see is a **hard ceiling**: when
you tap Buy, the fill re-prices and charges that total or less,
never more — if prices moved, you get the fresh quote before
anything is charged. Short on credits, or nobody is selling? The
sheet says so and points at the doors (sell loot, roam, refine,
research) instead of failing silently. Guests can open the sheet
and see the real prices; signing in is only asked at the Buy
button.

The same **+** rides the other shortfall lines in the game: the
[[Repair Board]]'s kit line and the [[Shield-Tech]] board's
Calibration Core line (for whichever kit type you have selected),
and the Garage's repair controls when the selected kit type can't
fully restore the vehicle. Everywhere it appears, the count is
the same kind of number — what you still need after the stock you
hold is spent, counted in standard-quality units — and the sheet
behind it is the same one. Patch Kits are a Vendor staple, so
both tabs can price them; Field and Master Patch Kits and
Calibration Cores are never vendor stock, so their sheets price
the Market only.

## The catalog: 88 recipes

The unified Workshop catalog holds **88 recipes**:

- **29 consumable recipes** — med kits, shield cells, ammo, ordnance,
  stims, scanners, and utility goods.
- **5 vehicle consumables** — Patch Kit, Field Patch Kit, Master Patch
  Kit, the crafted Fuel Cell, and the Calibration Core for
  [[Shield-Tech]] work.
- **8 refining recipes** — turn base materials into their refined forms
  on the crusher, and combine refined forms into composites in the
  furnace.
- **5 gear frames** — Weapon, Shield, Class Mod, Relic, and Ordnance.
- **4 craft relics** — named relics crafted like gear (see *Craft
  relics* below): the three former workshop tools — Precision
  Calipers, Servo Bench Rig, Reclaimer Head — plus the Prime
  Laboratory, the craft's ceiling carried on the hunter.
- **6 Manufacturer Standing exclusives** — one consumable per
  manufacturer, unlocked at rank 2 on that maker's ladder (see
  [[Manufacturer Standing]]).
- **3 Research batch recipes** — Shield Cells, Patch Kits, and Ordnance
  Charges made 3 at a time, gated behind the Production Systems course
  in [[Actions and Occupations|Research]]: fewer materials and less
  time per unit than three singles.
- **27 cosmetic recipes** — the [[Skins and Cosmetics]] catalog:
  26 scrap-led skin parts (no stats, ever) in the Skin Parts lane,
  plus the item-targeted **tint craft** run from the Tinting lane.
- **1 Chromaweave refine** — Myco-Steel Fiber + Void Essence woven
  into Chromaweave, the tint material (see [[Skins and Cosmetics]]).

## The Bench

There is exactly **one bench**, and it is yours from the start —
free, always ready, never locked. (The old Forge and Lab tiers
were retired: hunters who had bought one were refunded in full —
5,000 credits for the Forge, 30,000 credits + 10 VoidDust for the
Lab — and every recipe now runs on the one bench.) It runs **1 job
at a time with 5 queued** behind it; a Fixer keystone raises the
queue. The crusher and the furnace are stations on the same bench.

What the tiers used to sell — better craft quality — now lives on
**craft relics**, worn by the hunter like any relic:

## Craft relics

Four named relics carry workshop effects. They are crafted from
the Gear Frames lane at a **fixed Epic** rarity with **fixed
effects** — no roll, no temper, no catalyst — and they apply to
every job **while equipped** in your relic slot. Relics never wear
out, and a craft relic's effects can never be reforged away: they
are the relic's identity.

- **Precision Calipers** — rolled craft quality **×1.08**.
- **Servo Bench Rig** — craft unit durations **×0.85**.
- **Reclaimer Head** — refining yield **+5 points**, and a **10%
  bonus-unit chance** on consumable crafts.
- **Prime Laboratory** — rolled craft quality **×1.10** with **+5
  to the minimum roll**: the old Lab's gift, and the ceiling of
  the craft. Quality effects never stack past it — wearing two
  quality relics still caps at ×1.10/+5; the strongest duration,
  yield, and chance effects likewise combine only up to their own
  relic values.

The Bench card at the top of the Workshop lists all four with
their effects, owned or not.

Two recipe-card terms print as visible text on the card, never hidden:
**catalysts** raise a craft's quality bounds and run on the bench
as a baseline — their cost is their input (see below) — and a
**Rush Ticket** is escrowed for one job and runs it faster by the
ticket's quality — tickets are crafted under Utility recipes and
rotate through the vendor.

## Inputs: materials, essences, signatures

Recipes consume three families of inputs, earned by roaming (and all
tradable on [[The Market]]). Salvage is **not** a material supply
line — see Getting started below for what it actually pays. In the
Workshop's materials panel and on every recipe's input rows, each
input draws as its formed object — the same render it wears in your
inventory and on the Market — so goods are learned by shape and
color, and a refined input visibly reads as its base material,
aligned:

- **5 base materials** — Scrap Alloy, Ballistic Parts, Reactive
  Components, Tech, and Void Dust — plus their **5 refined
  forms** (Refined Alloy, Tempered Parts, Stabilized Cells, Woven
  Circuits, Condensed Void) and **3 composites** (Composite
  Plating, Circuit Lattice, Voidweave), which refining produces and
  gear frames consume.
- **5 essences** — one per non-Kinetic element (Ignition Fluid, Conduit
  Wire, Acid Compound, Cryo Gel, Void Essence). Each essence type
  trades on [[The Market]] as its own quantity commodity, so a crafter
  short on one element can buy exactly that essence instead of farming
  for it.
- **7 Tier-2 signature materials** — one sourced from each roaming
  area's home territory (Siltglass, Nitrocell Gel, Heartwood Resin,
  Forgeheart Ingots, Prism Shards, Magnetite Cores, Myco-Steel Fiber).
  Signatures are also the **catalysts**: adding a recipe's signature
  catalyst widens its quality roll (+10 to the minimum, +15 to the
  maximum).

Inputs are escrowed when a job is queued — they are spoken for until
the job finishes or you cancel it. A recipe's Scrap price is tuned
live, per recipe (see [[Economy]]): the card always shows the price
that will actually be charged, and the price a queued job started
at is the price it keeps — its card shows the escrow it is holding,
not today's price.

## Quality is real

A crafted consumable carries a **quality percentage**, rolled per batch
from a range set by the consumable type, widened by your crafting
mastery and any catalyst, and — with the Prime Laboratory relic
equipped — raised by +5 at the minimum before the ×1.10 is applied. Quality is capped at **175%**,
with 125% counting as high quality and 150% as perfect. The Workshop
prints the band key in the recipe list — Crude under 85, Rough 85+,
Standard 95+, Refined 106+, Superior 120+, Prototype 140+, Anomalous
160+ — and 100% is the Vendor's flat baseline: anything over it beats
store-bought. And quality is not a label: the engines read it. A 130%
Med Kit heals 130% of base, a 130% Patch Kit restores 130% of its base
condition, a 130% Fuel Cell loads 130 fuel units.

This is the crafter's whole edge over the NPC [[Vendor]]:

- The vendor sells a short list of **baseline staples** — Med Kit,
  Shield Cell, Ammo Pack, Ordnance Charge, Boss Scanner, Insurance
  Token, Luck Booster, Generic Fuel Cell, Patch Kit — always at a flat
  **100% quality**.
- **10 consumable types are vendor-excluded**: the vendor never stocks
  them at any quality. The Field and Master Patch Kits, the crafted
  Fuel Cell, the Calibration Core, and all six standing exclusives
  exist only because a crafter made them.
- Everything else the vendor doesn't stock is crafter-and-market
  supply too. If you want it above 100% — or in some cases at all —
  someone has to make it. That someone can be you.

## Gear crafting: capped at Epic

Gear frames produce real [[Gear]] in the slot of your choice, at a
rarity you choose — **capped at Epic**. Legendaries are found, not
made, and crafted gear never carries a [[Legendary Effects|legendary
effect]]. Frame times run from 90 minutes (Common/Uncommon) through
150 minutes (Rare) to 240 minutes (Epic); Epic frames additionally
need gear mastery level 10.

One exception bends the cap without breaking it: a gear-frame job
may escrow **1 legendary core per unit** as a
Legendary Core Catalyst, buying extra generation draws toward a better
roll. The core buys luck, never rarity — see [[Collections]] for where
cores come from.

A gear frame may instead escrow its **material catalyst** —
Forgeheart Ingots, 1 per unit — which raises the frame's quality
roll by the same +10 to the minimum and +15 to the maximum the
consumable catalysts give: every generation draw rolls inside the
raised bounds and the best roll is kept. The two catalysts are
mutually exclusive — a unit takes the core or the ingots, never both.

## Getting started

Open **Gear → Workshop**, pick a recipe you have the inputs for, set
crafting as your active action, and let the bench work. Roaming is
the beginner's supply line: areas drop the base materials and
essences their cards name. Salvage from the [[Gear]] tab pays **Scrap**
(and legendary cores when you break down a Legendary) — materials
come back only as an occasional single bonus material for Common
items once you hold the King of Scrap keystone, so treat salvage as
your Scrap income, never as a way to stock a specific material.
There is only one Scrap stock: what salvage pays is exactly what
skin parts, tints, reforge and rerolls draw from — and the same
stack you can list on [[The Market]], so a short bench can be
covered by buying Scrap instead of grinding for it.

Every material and essence the bench consumes is pictured with its
mark in [[Reference-Recipes]].
