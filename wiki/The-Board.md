# The Board

The Board is your home screen — the first thing you see, and the screen every session passes through. It lives on the **Home** tab: the app has four tabs — **Home, Gear, Market, Hunter** — and Home merges what used to be separate Board and Action tabs into one screen, with the action stage (below) at its centre. It is arranged so the things you check most are visible on the first paint, without scrolling. From top to bottom:

## The resource bar

Six chips, always on display:

| Chip | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Credits** | Your spendable credits |
| **Scrap** | Salvage material for crafting, repairs, and reforging |
| **Keys** | Cache keys — with your key fragments riding alongside as progress toward the next key (shown as fragments out of 5) |
| **Cores** | Legendary cores, earned by salvaging Legendaries — see [[Collections]] |
| **Loot** | How many items you hold, with your equipped count alongside (equipped out of 7 gear slots) |
| **Level** | Your hunter level |

The bar is display-only. There are no ads on this screen, ever. At the bar's right edge sits a small **gear icon** — the shortcut to Settings (whose home is Hunter → Account).

## The top bar: level, streak, contracts, weekly

Directly under the resource bar sit the claim chips:

- **The level button** takes most of the row: an **LV badge** with your level and a live **XP progress bar** showing your XP into the level, how much the level needs, and how much remains. Tapping it opens the level sheet with the full breakdown — unless a while-you-were-away recap exists, in which case the same tap opens the **Return Sheet** (below) and the level sheet is one tap further, from the Return Sheet's footer.
- **The streak flame** shows your current login-streak day. Tapping it opens the streak sheet with the 7-day ladder. When today's check-in has just paid out, the flame pulses with a **NEW** badge — an announcement that the reward already banked (watch your credits tick up in the Strip); opening the sheet and tapping **Back to the hunt** acknowledges it and clears the badge. You never need to tap it to check in: loading Home records the day by itself. See [[Leaderboards and Streaks]].
- **The contracts icon** (the clipboard, labelled Orders) opens the contracts panel. When contracts are ready to claim, it pulses and carries a badge with the **claimable count** — visible without opening anything. Guests see a **GUEST** badge instead — the why prints on the pill itself: guests can read the board, but claiming rewards requires signing in. See [[Getting Started]].
- **The weekly chip** (a crate, labelled Weekly) appears only while a weekly **Parts Crate** or **Signature Order** is claimable at the Rep Desk, and carries the claimable count. Tapping it opens the weekly claim sheet, where the offers can be claimed right there. See [[Manufacturer Standing]].

Claimable state is designed to be glanceable: if something is waiting for you, the top bar says so before you scroll anywhere.

## The stage

The centre of Home is **the stage**: your hunter, rendered with the weapon you actually have equipped, with a **gear count plate** at the top-right corner and a **status ribbon** along the bottom edge that names the current activity and carries your **stats band** (below). What just happened — drops, finds, kills — reads in the feed cards under the stage, not in the ribbon. No banner rides the art — the stage does not print the area's name; the doors that matter in the moment live in the workspace directly under it: in standby that is the Roam panel (the area's rates, one **Start Roam** bar, and **Change Area · Loadout** beneath it); while a boss encounter is open, the encounter panel carries its own fight and Change Boss controls. This is the Action workspace, living on Home itself: pick an area, tap Start Roam, and the rundown begins on the same screen.

The **stats band** is the ribbon's payload: your **effective stats for the action you are in**, live — swap gear, burn fuel, finish a buff and the numbers move. Roaming shows your damage per second and damage per tick first, then crit and element, your HP and mitigation, and the gathering faucet per tick (credits, scrap, XP, and your gear, fragment, and material find chances) with your vehicle's yield folded in. A boss fight shows your effective DPS against that boss, shield and armor break, and the boss's own DPS beside its recommended power; crafting shows the running job's bench, mastery, quality range, and time left; extraction shows the pass's gear factor against its damage. At standby the band shows the numbers your loadout *will* roam with, plus your offline rate and AFK cap — you can read your build before you start. Every number prints its **effective** value large; where your gear, skills, or vehicle lifted it above its base, the gain prints beside it smaller and green (base 100 DPS reading 150 prints **150 +50%**), and where something is costing you — an empty fuel tank's lost yield, say — the loss prints in red. Unmodified stats print plain. The band scrolls sideways rather than shrinking its type, and at its end sits a **Loadout** button that opens your loadout on the Gear tab, where gear changes live.

While a fight runs, each fighter's health sits at their feet, never across their face: your hunter's **HP plate** rides at his lower body on his side of the stage — his current and maximum health, with the bar — and the enemy's **HP plate** (its name and health bar) anchors under the enemy on the ground line. A boss's name rides its HP plate the same way.

Finds on the stage rise as what they actually are: a gear drop gets its rarity beam and item card; a cache-key fragment rises as the blue key shard; materials and essences rise as their own icons; a kill pays out with a scrap rise. The big cache crate never celebrates an ordinary roaming tick — roaming grants fragments, materials, and the base payout, while cache boxes and completed keys come from the cache lane and fragment settlement (five fragments make a key). See [[Caches]].

The weapon in your hunter's hands is always the exact weapon you have equipped — its type, make, and rolled parts, drawn by the same renderer that draws its card — held at the grip, with nothing else underneath it. The same hunter, with the same rule, steps into the boss arena: what you see on the Board is what you fight with. Equip nothing and the hands are empty.

Your equipped gear is worn the same way — mounted on the figure itself, moving as he moves. Your shield projects from his support arm as its own behavior glyph (the starburst, ward, and so on), tinted by its element; your relic hangs at his chest framed by its rarity; the pauldron on his shoulder wears your class mod's tree; and the consumables slotted for the fight ride his belt. Swap any piece and the figure on the stage is wearing the new one — unequip it and that read is simply gone.

Under the gear, the figure itself is dressed by your [[Skins-and-Cosmetics|skin parts]]: wear a crafted part and it replaces the matching piece of the standard suit on the stage, in the boss arena, everywhere your hunter appears. Parts are pure looks — the stage hunter fights exactly as hard in a shaped Bulwark set as in the stock suit.

Changing area lives one tap from the stage: the mode rail's **Area** stop and the workspace panels' **Change Area** buttons both open the area picker. If you are enrolled in a [[Progression|Research]] course, its chip sits just above the stage, because research runs alongside your action rather than replacing it.

## The mode rail

Under the stage runs the **mode rail** — six stops, one tap each: **Area · Roam · Boss · Endless · Extract · Craft**. The lit stop is the mode whose workspace is open below. Endless, Extraction, and Crafting are full stops on the rail; the **Switch Action** sheet still offers every mode it always did, Salvage included — the rail is the fast door, the sheet is the complete one.

The action controls themselves float **on the stage**: the **Switch Action** chip sits at the stage's top-left corner in every state — it is the sheet's one door — and while an action is running, a **Stop** chip joins it. There is no separate controls row; the space under the rail belongs to the workspace.

Below the rail sits the **lit workspace** for the current mode, then the **consumable belt**: your active consumable slots, above the fold. Tap a slot to open the ledger and manage what is loaded.

## Event pop-ups

Live tick events surface as small pop-up cards in a fixed lane above
the bottom navigation. They are glance-only: each card is a single
headline carrying the outcome — the gain or loss and its subject
(`+1 BallisticParts`, `-12 HP`, `+12.5cr · +3 scrap · +16 XP`, a drop's
name) — with no narrative line under it. Inside the headline the
number is the hero: gain values print in bright gain-green at full
weight, damage taken prints in red, and the words beside a value
sit quieter. At most two cards show at
once, newest first, and each clears a few seconds after it appears;
newer events replace older ones rather than walling off the screen.
Gear drops follow the rarity law inside the lane: Common and
Uncommon drops use the compact card, Rare drops add their rarity
stamp in rarity color, and Epic and Legendary drops keep the full
two-line card (their moment on the stage itself is the separate,
rarity-scaled loot celebration). The complete event — what happened,
where, and from whom — is never lost: the same events print with
their full detail in the Now rows in the deck below and in the Action
screen's reward stream.

One exception to the popping: gains earned during a brief app
switch (under 15 seconds away) never pop on your return. The
action resumes the moment you are back, and those few ticks fold
straight into the Now rows and the reward stream below — the
record is complete, the return is quiet. Drops still pop and
still take their stage moment; only the routine gain cards fold.

## The deck

Everything below the fold is the **deck**, in a fixed order:

1. **Launchers** — the directory doors: **Dispatch** and **Crack Cache** first, then **Slots** (the card lands you right on the wheel at the Market rail stop), then the **Loadout Bench** shortcut — which only appears while your loadout is incomplete (fewer than 7 of 7 gear slots filled).
2. **Weekly claimables** — the weekly Parts Crate and Signature Order offers, with their claim buttons. This panel only exists while something is claimable; an empty week leaves no empty box behind.
3. **Now** — one merged feed: your latest activity plus the world pulse (current activity, level progress, boss progress, best weapon, bench state).
4. **Hunter Totals** — your hunter's combat numbers — health, damage, and the rest — computed from your real gear, skills, and buffs. Its **Sources** button opens a full breakdown of where every number comes from.

## While you were away

Your hunter keeps working while you're gone, up to your offline cap (see [[Progression]]). When you return, the earnings settle automatically and the **Return Sheet** waits behind the level button — tap it to read the recap. While an unread recap is waiting, the level button pulses and carries an **AWAY** badge.

The Return Sheet presents the absence in the loot language: a **totals row** with the headline earnings, the absence's **best drop as a poster card**, the itemised rewards below, and one **Collect** button. The primer about **notifications** rides the sheet's footer — a single row inside the recap is the one place the app will ever offer to turn push notifications on, at the moment you can see exactly what a notification would have told you. The level sheet itself now holds level specifics only; while a recap exists it carries a door back to the Return Sheet.

One exception: if you were away **less than five minutes**, the recap never presents itself. The settlement still lands in full — it simply stays tucked behind the level button for you to read if you want it, instead of interrupting a quick check-in.
